Cargo vaults hold **real-world assets** as receipted, pledged inventory: goods with a standard grade, a deep reference price and a short path to a buyer. Soft commodities such as cocoa, coffee and sugar, metals such as gold, and anything else that passes the [eight tests](#eight-tests-for-any-commodity). Each asset is its own vault, with its own lots, NAV and token.

Physical assets are hard for four reasons: volatility, basis between physical and futures prices, quality decay and legal reach. Cargo's structure answers each one, and the eight tests decide whether an asset qualifies at all.

## Custody and verification

The same controls apply in every vault:

- **Collateral manager:** independent, with sole release authority. Goods leave only against payment.
- **Verification:** an independent inspector weighs and grades every lot. A discrepancy above 0.5% suspends the lot until it is made good, and triggers a recount.
- **Reporting:** weekly stock reports hashed on-chain; lot IDs, weights and grades public.
- **Insurance:** all-risks stock cover on every lot.

## Legal structure

| Layer | Instrument |
| :-- | :-- |
| Title | A bankruptcy-remote, limited-recourse Vault SPV owns each lot from the moment it is receipted |
| Security | A registered pledge over the goods, with the collateral manager as third-party holder |
| Enforcement | One security agent for all holders |
| Sale and cash | Goods are sold through licensed exporters or dealers |
| Originator retention | 10% of each lot as subordinated shares (first loss) |
| Offering | Private, to eligible non-US professional or institutional investors (Regulation S). The Vault SPV is a Seychelles company; Seychelles residents and any jurisdictions in the vault's term sheet are excluded |

## Eight tests for any commodity

An asset becomes a Cargo vault only if it passes all eight:

| # | Test | Means |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 1 | Standard grade | An exchange or trade-association specification |
| 2 | Deep reference price | A liquid future or a published index |
| 3 | Storability | Shelf life well beyond the holding period |
| 4 | Independent custody | A collateral manager with sole release authority |
| 5 | Perfectable security | A registrable pledge or title where the goods are stored |
| 6 | Liquid physical exit | At least three approved buyers able to take a lot within 10 days |
| 7 | Insurable | All-risk stock and transit cover at commercial rates |
| 8 | Traceable | Lot-level origin documents, including EUDR data where required |

## Which assets fit

| Class | Examples | Reference | Fit |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| ICE London softs | Cocoa, robusta coffee, white sugar | ICE Futures Europe | Strong: the Genesis Vault is cocoa |
| ICE US softs | Arabica coffee, raw sugar, cotton | ICE Futures US | Strong |
| Precious metals | Gold, silver | LBMA | Strong: low volatility, vaulted |
| Base metals | Copper, aluminium | LME | Strong: warrant system, low decay |
| Grains and oilseeds | Wheat, corn, soybeans | CBOT, Euronext | Good: thin margins, large lots |
| Energy | Crude, refined products | Brent, WTI | Harder: storage and licensing |
| Crops without futures | Cashew, shea, timber | None or thin | Weak |

Vaults are added one at a time. Coffee and sugar share the Genesis Vault's rails, so they are the nearest candidates. See [Vaults](/products/vaults#vault-lines).
